**B-628-13 — a renewal decline erases the dispute cycle, then the renewal payment settles the disputed payment**
- **Where:**
  - The dispute cycle is identified only by the `last_failure_reason` marker:
    - `isDisputeCycleOpen` (dunning-v2.service.ts:1139-1147)
    - `isDisputeCycle` (client-billing.service.ts:315-317)
    - the `applyImmediateClear` guard (dunning-v2.service.ts:930-937)
    - the `resolveDunningOnPaid` guard (checkout-webhook-handler.service.ts:1201-1213)
  - The v1 path overwrites that marker on every later `invoice.payment_failed`: checkout-webhook-handler.service.ts:1293 passes `reason: inv.last_payment_error?.message`, and dunning.service.ts:252 writes `last_failure_reason: input.reason ?? existing.last_failure_reason` onto the same active row.
- **Counterexample:** a dispute opens a reversal cycle, then the next renewal declines. That is common, because a disputed card is often reissued.
  - The marker becomes `"Your card has insufficient funds."`, and `isDisputeCycleOpen` turns false.
  - The client then pays that renewal. `invoice.paid` → `resolveDunningOnPaid` no longer sees a dispute cycle, so v1 resolves the row and `applyImmediateClear` restores access while dispute dp_1 is still open.
  - This is the exact rule B-628-8 fixed ("a renewal payment does not settle a disputed payment"). The lockout timeline (`tryLock` :832) and the client status `kind` also flip from dispute to payment.
- **Probe:** red, run at 9b48d91e. R8 and the merge leave every dunning, webhook and dispute file unchanged. [ci-lane run 37143152376](https://github.com/BradleyGleavePortfolio/growth-project-backend/actions/runs/37143152376). It is the existing B-628-8 harness plus one case.
  - The control "locks on its Day 10 ... a renewal payment and a card update do not settle it" passes.
  - The probe fails: after the decline, `last_failure_reason: "Your card has insufficient funds."` and `dispute_cycle_open: false`. After paid, `status: "resolved"`.
  - Probe source: `ops/aud-115/AUD-OPUS-MONEY-2/probe628-dunning-r3-money-truth-e2e.spec.ts`.
- **Same root, other order:** a dispute that arrives while a payment cycle is active records its obligation and returns `cycle_already_active` (dunning-v2.service.ts:1046-1048). Paying the renewal then resolves the cycle with that obligation still open, and nothing reopens it.
- **Fix rule:** "dispute outstanding" must come from durable state the v1 failure path cannot overwrite.
  - Decide it from `DunningDisputeObligation` (any `open`/`lost` row for the purchase), or from a dedicated column, in `isDisputeCycleOpen`, `isDisputeCycle`, `applyImmediateClear`, `resolveDunningOnPaid` and `tryLock`.
  - Never let `recordFailure` replace the reversal marker on an active dispute cycle.
  - When a payment cycle resolves with an outstanding obligation, keep or convert it to the dispute cycle.
  - Pin both orders with the probe above (decline after dispute; dispute during a payment cycle, then paid).

**C-628-14 — a first-call 429 is credited as this update's payment** (client-billing.service.ts:1004-1016)
- `payOne` treats `httpStatus === 429` as a lost reply. If the immediate re-read shows the invoice paid, the line is `paid` with its cents.
- A 429 means Stripe never ran the call, so whatever paid the invoice was not this card update. R7 fixed that same attribution for later reconciliation (the same-key replay proof), but not for this immediate path. Money is not double-collected; only the reply and the journal over-attribute.
- **Fix rule:** on 429, report `already_paid` (0 cents) when the re-read is paid, or settle through `replayPay`.

**C-628-15 — a 409 `idempotency_key_in_use` on the first call is reported as `failed` with "nothing was charged"** (client-billing.service.ts:1037-1045, copy :1319-1323)
- A 409 means a request with the same key is still running, and that request may charge. This is reachable only when the 120 s lease (:73) expired during a call, so it is rare.
- **Fix rule:** classify a 409 like the replay does, as `uncertain` / `PAYMENT_RESULT_UNKNOWN`, and never as definitive.
