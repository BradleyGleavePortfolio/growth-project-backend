### B-680-1: a stale subscription snapshot revokes a paid native plan, and the client is then sold a second subscription

- **Where.** `src/checkout/checkout-webhook-handler.service.ts:1002` (`entitlementActive = subscriptionGrantsAccess(purchase, sub)`) and the write at `:1020-1032` apply whatever status the event payload carries. Every native subscription is created `default_incomplete`, so its `customer.subscription.created` always carries `status: incomplete`.
- **Counterexample (probe, red).** First invoice paid: `invoice.paid` (prefetched subscription `active`) grants access. The `created` event's first delivery failed; its retry arrives next and sets the row to `incomplete`, `entitlement_active: false`. Same result when the grant came from `customer.subscription.updated(active)`. Nothing repairs it until the next renewal event (about a month). A day later the client taps subscribe again with a new key: `decide()` (`src/checkout/subscription-checkout.service.ts:684-715`) sees no live row and no open attempt younger than 23 h, reserves, and mints a second Stripe subscription (`stripe._subs.size` = 2). The client is billed twice for one plan.
- **Minimal fix rule.** Stripe never moves a subscription back to `incomplete`, and `canceled` / `incomplete_expired` are terminal. In `applySubscriptionUpdated`, for a recurring row: (1) an `incomplete` payload for a purchase that is entitled or whose status left `pending`/`incomplete`/`payment_failed` is stale: claim it, write nothing; (2) a row that is `canceled`, `expired` or `incomplete_expired` is never re-granted by `created`/`updated` (covers C-680-3). Equivalent alternative: for native rows, read the live subscription out of the transaction (as `invoice.paid` already does via `invoiceSubscription`) and apply that instead of the payload. Add both orders as regressions.

### B-680-2: a late first-invoice decline puts a paid plan (or an ended attempt) into dunning

- **Where.** `src/checkout/checkout-webhook-handler.service.ts:1464` decides "first attempt" with `isNeverEntitledAttempt(purchase)` (`:71-79`), i.e. from today's row state, not from the invoice. `:1278-1288` writes the decline text into `last_error` for any recurring row.
- **Counterexamples (probes, red).** (a) Card declined in the PaymentSheet, second card paid, `invoice.paid` granted; the decline (`invoice.payment_failed`, `billing_reason: subscription_create`) is delivered after it: the paid plan becomes `past_due`, `last_error` shows the decline, and `dunning.recordFailure` opens a dunning window (client reminders, grace-end cancel). (b) The first-invoice `payment_intent.payment_failed` delivered after `invoice.paid` leaves "Your card was declined." on an active plan; `last_error` is returned by the client and coach purchase selects. (c) An abandoned attempt that Stripe expired (`updated` to `incomplete_expired`) or that checkout retired (`expired`), then the late decline: status becomes `past_due` and dunning starts for a plan nobody bought. Control: a `subscription_cycle` renewal decline on a running plan still enters dunning (green).
- **Minimal fix rule.** `billing_reason === 'subscription_create'` means first attempt regardless of row state: never `past_due`, never dunning, and no `last_error` write once the purchase is entitled or ended. In the PI early return, write `last_error` only while `isNeverEntitledAttempt(purchase)`. This is the same rule as the dunning stack's `isNeverEntitledPaymentAttempt(purchase, billingReason)`; adopting its signature here (keeping this PR's `trial_started_at` guard) makes the later unification mechanical.
